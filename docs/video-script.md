# Video script: Smoke Smart Spokane (target 4:00 to 4:30)

**Tools:** Zoom (share screen and record), PowerPoint or Canva video export, or Loom.
**Setup:** Open the website. Keep the chatbot ready. Turn on captions.
**Pace:** About 130 words per minute. This script is about 560 words.

---

## 0:00 to 0:35 | The health issue
**On screen:** Home page hero, then the four stat figures.

> On August 1, 2026, three fires started around Spokane and became the Spokane Complex Fire. More than 900 structures were damaged or destroyed. About 60,000 people were told to evacuate. Our thoughts are with every family who lost a home.
> The smoke reached everyone. By mid-August, the air was unhealthy on 14 days, close to the record of 16. My campaign is Smoke Smart Spokane. Its message is three words: Check. Clean. Cut back.

## 0:35 to 1:05 | Audience and goals
**On screen:** "Who makes the call" section, then the objectives table.

> Our main audience is parents of school-age children. About one in five county residents is under eighteen. Our second audience is outdoor workers and their supervisors. Coaches, teachers and nurses are key influencers.
> We have three goals: help people understand the AQI, help them act, and build supports that last. For example, we want six school districts to adopt a tiered plan by August 2027.

## 1:05 to 1:50 | Socioecological model and health literacy
**On screen:** Five-level diagram, then the health literacy section.

> We work at five levels at once. At the individual level, people learn their number. At the interpersonal level, coaches and crew leads model the plan. At the organizational level, schools and employers adopt clear plans. At the community level, we partner with health agencies. At the policy level, we ask districts to match local health advice at AQI 101 to 150.
> The levels reinforce each other. A parent who knows the number still needs a school rule that matches.
> For health literacy, every material pairs a color, a number and one action. We use plain language and teach-back. The school poster and worksite flyer also come in Spanish. We use gain-framed messages, because checking the air is a prevention behavior.

## 1:50 to 2:20 | Logic model
**On screen:** Logic model.

> This logic model links inputs to outcomes. Inputs are partners, funding and evidence. Activities include social media, school and worksite toolkits, trainings and the chatbot. Outputs are things we can count. Short-term outcomes are knowledge and checking the AQI. Long-term outcomes are lasting habits, a shared county standard and fewer smoke-related asthma visits.

## 2:20 to 3:20 | Walk through the materials
**On screen:** Materials section. Show each image for about 10 seconds.

> On social media, Post 1 is "Know your number," a color-coded AQI card. Post 2 shows how to make a clean-air room in three steps.
> For schools, the Smoke Day Play Plan poster has three tiers: green, orange and red. The orange tier protects children with asthma at AQI 101 to 150. It also comes in Spanish.
> For workplaces, our flyer turns Washington's rule into two numbers: 69 and 101. At 69, employers must train workers. At 101, they must provide respirators for voluntary use. Supervisors get a five-minute toolbox talk.

## 3:20 to 4:10 | AI chatbot demo
**On screen:** Chatbot. Type or click these:

1. Type `AQI 130` and read the answer.
2. Click "Do masks protect me from smoke?"
3. Click "I was affected by the fire. Where can I get help?"
4. Click "Quiz me" and answer one question.

> The AI element is a health FAQ chatbot. It matches each question to a reviewed answer and names the source. It gives an action for any AQI number, it flags emergencies, and it does not give medical advice. The quiz uses teach-back to check understanding. It runs in the browser, so it stores no data.

## 4:10 to 4:30 | Close
**On screen:** Evaluation table, then the hero.

> We would measure success with pre and post surveys, school and employer adoption, and chatbot ratings. Smoke Smart Spokane helps families check the number, clean their air and cut back, so they stay healthy through smoke season. Thank you.
